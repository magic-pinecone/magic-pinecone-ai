Article 1  The National Central University Regulations on Common Course
  Selection and Requirements (hereinafter referred to as the Regulations)
  are stipulated to cultivate students’ literacy of liberal arts and achieve
  the objectives of general education.
Article 2  The required subjects and number of credits of common courses for
  undergraduate students are listed as follows:
  Subject Names / Course Areas  Numbers of Credits
  Chinese  5
  Foreign Languages  6
  Physical Education  0
  Student Service Learning  0
  General Education  Required Core
  Program  Courses  14
  Elective courses
  Total Number of Credits  25
Article 3  Courses in Chinese literacy include Chinese (A), an advanced semester-
  based three-credit Chinese course, and “Chinese (B): Chinese Writing,”
  a semester-based two-credit course. Overseas Chinese students and
  international students should take the full-year five-credit course
  “Chinese for Overseas Chinese Students.” Students who take the
  “Chinese for Overseas Chinese Students” are not required to take
  “Chinese (B): Chinese Writing.”
Article 4  Course requirements of foreign languages should be subject to the
  regulations of the departments (and degree programs). The department
  should select one of the following requirements and specify it in the
  additional remark column in the list of curricula:
  1. “Freshman English”;
  2. English-related courses provided by the Department of English of
  National Central University (NCU);
  3.  courses of a second or third foreign language in basic or upper-
  basic levels that are provided or recognized by the Language
  Center at NCU—students must complete at least six credits of the
  courses of the same foreign language.
  Students who are exempted from taking “Freshman English” will
  directly earn the credits of this course, and the credits will count toward
  the number of their credits for graduation.
Article 5  Undergraduate students are required to take physical education courses
  for five semesters (Freshman Physical Education for the first and
  second semester in the freshman year and courses in specific sports for
  three semesters). The credits of the courses will not count toward the
  number of credits for graduation. Students are not allowed to take the
  courses with the same title for certain types of sports continually
  (students of a varsity sports team and adapted physical education are
  excluded).
  Students who have completed the aforementioned physical education
  courses are allowed to select a one-credit physical education course, and
  the credit of this course will not count toward the number of credits for
  graduation.
  Undergraduate students admitted via the Independent Admission of
  Students with Excellent Athletic Performance should complete
  Freshman Physical Education (for the first and second semester) and
  the physical education courses of the varsity teams to which they belong
  for three semesters, which are five semesters in total. The students
  should complete the aforementioned required courses before
  graduation. Students of special cases should apply in written documents
  and seek approval from the Vice President for Academic Affairs. Once
  the application is approved, the course requirements for the students
  may not be subject to the preceding rule.
Article 6  Undergraduate students are required to take Student Service Learning
  (with separate course serial numbers) for two semesters. The course
  credits will not count toward the number of credits for graduation.
Article 7  Undergraduate students should take and complete 14 credits in the
  general education program. Students should select at least one of the
  four major fields below for the required core courses: humanities and
  thoughts, physical science, applied science, and trends of thought and
  phenomena of society. Students’ selected major fields should meet the
  requirements regulated by the students' departments (or degree
  programs). If a student’s case applies to Article 7, Paragraph 4 of the
  Regulations, the student may have an exemption from taking courses in
  the general education program. As students are selecting courses in the
  general education program, they will be given priority in selecting
  cross-disciplinary courses. (Students of the College of Science, the
  College of Engineering, the College of Earth Sciences, the College of
  Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, and the College of
  Health Sciences and Technology may have priority over selecting the
  courses in the fields of humanities and thought and trends of thought
  and phenomena of the society. Students of the Colleges of Liberal Arts,
  the College of Hakka Studies, and the School of Management may have
  priority over selecting the courses in the fields of physical science and
  applied science).
  For students who take “professional courses that can be recognized for
  credits in the general education program,” the earned credits of such
  professional courses will count toward the number of credits of elective
  courses in the general education program first. If the earned credits have
  satisfied the credit requirement of the elective courses in the general
  education program, the extra credits can be regarded as the credits of
  the elective courses of other departments. However, if the professional
  courses are regarded as the student's major, the credits of those courses
  cannot count toward the number of credits of elective courses in the
  general education program.
  Students who complete taking the required courses of certain second
  professional specialties approved and recognized by the General
  Education Center may apply for a credit waiver for up to six credits of
  elective courses in the general education program. Students should refer
  to the webpage of the Division of Curriculum for the most updated list
  of the courses for waiving the elective courses in the general education
  program.
  Students who complete taking any credit programs or required courses
  of second professional specialties and these taken courses include eight
  credits or more (of the core required or elective courses) recognized in
  the general education program may apply for an exemption from taking
  all the courses in the general education program with the total credits
  earned in credit programs or second professional specialties.
  Students who meet the criteria listed in the preceding two paragraphs
  should meet the required number of credits for graduation regulated by
  their departments.
  Students who take and pass the micro courses in self-directed learning
  may have up to two credits of microcourses count toward the number
  of credits of elective courses in the general education program.
Article 8  The Regulations apply to students who enrolled in and after the 111th
  academic year.
Article 9  The Regulations have been approved at the NCU Academic Affairs
  Meeting prior to their announcement and implementation. Any future
  amendments should follow the same administrative procedures.
